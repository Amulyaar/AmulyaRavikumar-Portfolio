01 RECOMMENDATION SYSTEMS / EXPERIMENTATION

Recommendation
Health Audit

A ranking signal is only useful if you know what caused it — and what it might cost the ecosystem.

I analyzed 1.02M impressions across 102K recommendation slates in the public RARD dataset. Rank 1 had 0.36% observed CTR versus 0.14% for ranks 2–10. That pattern suggests a question, not an answer: is the difference driven by position, relevance, or both?

THE PRODUCT MOVE

Proposed a randomized rank-swap experiment among eligible items to isolate position effects while tracking engagement, content coverage, and creator exposure as guardrails.

THE QUESTION

How much of an item’s engagement reflects its quality, and how much reflects where it appears? An observational comparison cannot separate these effects.

THE PROPOSED TEST

Randomly swap eligible positions while keeping candidate selection stable. Compare click behavior between treatment and control, then inspect whether any gain comes with reduced discovery breadth or uneven creator exposure.

THE PM LESSON

Optimize the discovery system, not a single click metric. I would pair the primary engagement measure with safeguards for coverage, diversity, and creator opportunity before recommending rollout.

Status: analysis completed; randomized test proposed, not run. The CTR figures are descriptive observations.

    NOTE 01 / COLD START
    ↗

    How do we earn a new user’s second session?

    Start with a lightweight interest signal, then make the first feed broad enough to learn. I’d look beyond first-session watch time to return rate and how quickly the system discovers a user’s interests.

    HYPOTHESIS → TEST → LEARN
    NOTE 02 / CREATOR OPPORTUNITY
    ◎

    Can a feed get better while more creators get seen?

    Explore controlled exposure for promising new creators. Measure viewer response alongside the share of creators receiving meaningful reach, so short-term engagement does not hide a narrowing supply ecosystem.

    ENGAGEMENT + EXPOSURE
    NOTE 03 / TRUST
    ✳

    When does personalization become repetition?

    Test how much topic variety changes satisfaction over time. I’d use repeat visits and explicit negative feedback as guardrails, because a feed can hold attention today and feel stale tomorrow.
